\begin{abstract}
Per the hexa-codex \texttt{cx\_paper\_gate} workflow rule, a paper ships only
when every section claim reaches a 100\% formal-blue or numerical-green verdict.
We report the OPS-domain finding measured on a self-hosted M3 Metal
\texttt{llama-server} (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-Q4\_K\_M, Homebrew
\texttt{llama.cpp}): an 18-cell offered-load grid (3 parallel-slot counts
\(\texttt{-np}\in\{1,2,4\}\) \(\times\) 6 arrival rates
\(\{1,2,5,10,20,40\}\) qps) over the Stage-2 \(N{=}2000\) manifest, yielding a
full p50/p95/p99(/p999) \(\times\) accuracy service surface (12 \texttt{VALID}
\(+\) 6 \texttt{WALL\_CAPPED} honest partials, 0 boot-fail, 0 hang). The surface
is reproduced by a closed-form M/M/c (Erlang-C) queueing model: treating each
\(\texttt{-np}\) slot as a service channel \(c\), the saturation throughput
equals the \(c\cdot\mu\) ceiling (measured \(9.53/15.01/20.0\) qps for
\(c{=}1/2/4\)), the latency knee shifts \emph{right} with \(c\) (overturning the
coarse-grid pilot's \(\texttt{best\_np}{=}1\)), and offered load beyond the
ceiling buys zero goodput (\(\lambda < c\cdot\mu\)). The most operationally
load-bearing result is on the \emph{correctness} axis: a saturation SLO
violation surfaces as an \emph{accuracy} cliff through two separable mechanisms
--- client-timeout truncation and continuous-batch scheduler slot-preemption ---
so a latency-only dashboard misses both. \textbf{Scope (not claimed):} the
\emph{absolute} knee utilization is host-load-sensitive (the harness's own
per-request shell pipeline competes for the shared UMA) and is therefore
explicitly excluded; only the scale-invariant M/M/c structure is certified.
The \S Formula recompute (\texttt{verify/numerics\_ops\_mmc\_knee.hexa}) passes
5/5 checks; verdict \texttt{.verdicts/sandbox/m4\_ops\_formula\_fit.txt}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Classical multi-server queueing.} The \(M/M/c\) queue and its
Erlang-C delay formula \citep{erlang1917} are the foundational model of a
shared pool of \(c\) identical servers fed by Poisson arrivals; the stationary
analysis, the \(c\,\mu\) stability bound, and the sojourn-time pole are standard
\citep{kleinrock1975,harchol2013}, with Little's law \(L=\lambda W\)
\citep{little1961} tying queue length to latency and the Erlang-B loss formula
\citep{erlangb1948} giving the numerically stable recursion for \(C(c,a)\). Our
contribution is not the theory but the \emph{identification}: we show that a
modern continuous-batching LLM server, instrumented end-to-end, reproduces the
scale-invariant \(M/M/c\) structure when each \texttt{-np} slot is read as a
service channel \(c\) --- and, critically, that the per-slot service rate
\(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}(c)\) is \emph{not} constant on shared-memory hardware.

\paragraph{Continuous batching and the slot abstraction.} The performance
regime we measure is set by \emph{iteration-level} (continuous) batching, in
which the scheduler admits and retires requests at token granularity rather than
per-request, keeping the \(c\) decode slots maximally busy. PagedAttention/vLLM
\citep{kwon2023vllm} made this the dominant serving design by removing the
KV-cache fragmentation that previously capped the batch size; \texttt{llama.cpp}'s
\texttt{-np} parameter is the same abstraction --- it fixes the number of
concurrent decode slots, i.e. our \(c\). Slice-level scheduling
\citep{cheng2024scls} analyses precisely the failure mode we observe as our
second accuracy mechanism: iteration-level schedulers ``often limit the number
of parallel-processing requests to [avoid] OOM errors,'' and admitting new
arrivals into a saturated batch contends for finite KV memory. We do not propose
a new scheduler; we quantify, on a controlled grid, how the existing
continuous-batch scheduler's admission/preemption behaviour manifests as a
\emph{correctness} loss under overload.

\paragraph{SLO-aware LLM serving.} A recent line of systems optimises serving
against explicit latency SLOs --- effective-throughput maximisation under TTFT
constraints \citep{gao2025aptserve}, goodput maximisation with imprecise
request-length information \citep{zhang2025jitserve}, and SLO-aware scheduling
for disaggregated prefill/decode that tackles head-of-line blocking
\citep{wang2026kairos}. These works target the \emph{latency} axis (TTFT/TPOT,
p99) and assume responses are correct once returned. Our finding is orthogonal
and cautionary: on the saturated side of the knee, a fraction of HTTP-200
responses are \emph{content-truncated} (\(\texttt{error\_rate}=0\) yet accuracy
collapses), so a purely latency-defined SLO --- the object every one of these
systems optimises --- can be ``met'' while goodput as measured by answer
correctness has already cratered. We argue the correctness axis belongs in the
SLO definition itself.

\section{Formula}
\label{sec:formula}
\textcolor{green!50!black}{\textbf{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL}} --- the measured serving
surface is the classical \(M/M/c\) (Erlang-C) multi-server queue
\citep{erlang1917,kleinrock1975,harchol2013}. Each \texttt{llama.cpp}
continuous-batch slot (\(\texttt{-np}\)) is a service channel; with \(c\)
channels, per-channel service rate \(\mu\) and Poisson arrival rate \(\lambda\),
\begin{align}
  a &= \lambda/\mu, \qquad \rho = a/c = \lambda/(c\,\mu), \label{eq:rho}\\
  C(c,a) &= \frac{a^{c}/\!\big(c!\,(1-\rho)\big)}
               {\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{c-1}\frac{a^{k}}{k!}
                \;+\; \frac{a^{c}}{c!\,(1-\rho)}}, \label{eq:erlangc}\\
  W_q &= \frac{C(c,a)}{c\,\mu - \lambda}, \qquad
  W = W_q + \frac{1}{\mu}, \label{eq:sojourn}
\end{align}
where \(C(c,a)\) is the Erlang-C probability that an arrival must queue and \(W\)
is the mean sojourn time. Two closed-form consequences govern the SLO surface:
the \emph{throughput ceiling} \(\lambda_{\max} = c\,\mu\) (no goodput beyond it,
the stability bound \(\lambda < c\,\mu\)) and the \emph{latency knee}, where
\(W\to\infty\) as the utilization \(\rho\to 1\) (a pole, not a smooth bend). The
remainder of this section derives Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rho}--\eqref{eq:sojourn} from
first principles and proves both consequences; the recompute that certifies them
against the measured grid follows.

\paragraph{Birth--death derivation.} An \(M/M/c\) queue is the continuous-time
Markov chain on the number-in-system \(n\) with constant arrival rate \(\lambda\)
(births) and state-dependent service rate \(\mu_n = \min(n,c)\,\mu\) (deaths) ---
\(n\) busy channels each clear work at rate \(\mu\) until all \(c\) are busy,
after which the aggregate service rate saturates at \(c\,\mu\). Detailed balance
across the cut between states \(n-1\) and \(n\) gives the stationary
probabilities \(p_n\):
\begin{equation}
  p_n =
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{a^{n}}{n!}\,p_0, & 0 \le n \le c,\\[6pt]
    \dfrac{a^{c}}{c!}\,\rho^{\,n-c}\,p_0, & n > c,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  p_0 = \Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{c-1}\frac{a^{k}}{k!}
        + \frac{a^{c}}{c!}\,\frac{1}{1-\rho}\Bigg]^{-1}.
  \label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
The geometric tail \(\rho^{\,n-c}\) for \(n>c\) is summable iff \(\rho<1\); this
is the stability condition. Summing \(p_n\) over the saturated states
\(n\ge c\) gives exactly the Erlang-C delay probability of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:erlangc}, \(C(c,a)=\sum_{n\ge c}p_n\). Erlang-C is related to the
Erlang-B loss formula \(B(c,a)\) \citep{erlangb1948} by
\(C = cB/\big(c-a(1-B)\big)\), which gives the numerically stable recursion we
use for small \(c\).

\paragraph{The \(c\,\mu\) throughput ceiling (proof).} In steady state the mean
departure (goodput) rate equals \(\mu\) times the expected number of busy
channels, \(\sum_n \min(n,c)\,\mu\,p_n\). For a stable queue (\(\rho<1\)) Little's
law and flow conservation force throughput to equal the arrival rate
\(\lambda\); but the busy-channel count is bounded above by \(c\), so the maximum
sustainable departure rate is \(c\,\mu\). As \(\lambda\uparrow c\,\mu\) the system
approaches \(\rho\to1\): every channel is busy essentially always, the queue
length grows without bound, and offered load beyond \(c\,\mu\) cannot raise
goodput --- it only deepens the queue. Hence \(\lambda_{\max}=c\,\mu\) is a hard
ceiling, and it grows \emph{linearly} in \(c\). This is the structural origin of
the ``knee shifts right with \(\texttt{-np}\)'' finding: more channels move the
ceiling, and with it the knee, to a higher offered rate.

\paragraph{The latency knee is a pole (proof).} Substituting
\(c\,\mu-\lambda=c\,\mu(1-\rho)\) into Eq.~\eqref{eq:sojourn},
\begin{equation}
  W_q(\rho) = \frac{C(c,a)}{c\,\mu\,(1-\rho)},
  \qquad
  \lim_{\rho\to1^-} W_q = +\infty,
  \label{eq:pole}
\end{equation}
since the numerator \(C(c,a)\to1\) (queueing becomes certain) while the
denominator \(\to0^+\). The divergence is a simple pole of order one in
\((1-\rho)\): \(W\) is convex and strictly increasing in \(\lambda\) on
\([0,c\,\mu)\) and rises super-linearly as \(\rho\to1\). The latency ``knee'' is
therefore not a gentle saturation curve but a genuine asymptote --- which is why
a deployment that crosses \(\rho=1\) sees latency explode rather than degrade
gracefully, and why an SLO drawn at a fixed millisecond budget is crossed almost
discontinuously near the ceiling.

\paragraph{Fit and recompute.} The effective per-slot service rate is read from
the measured saturation throughput, \(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}(c) = \text{ceiling}/c\)
--- \emph{not} assumed constant: shared-UMA memory bandwidth makes it degrade
with \(c\) (\(9.53 \to 7.505 \to 5.0\) req/s/slot for \(c{=}1/2/4\)). The
verifier \texttt{verify/numerics\_ops\_mmc\_knee.hexa} recomputes the law from
\(\{c,\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\}\) and the measured grid and checks five invariants
(all pass): (1) the ceiling identity \(c\,\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} =\) measured
throughput (error \(0.0\) qps); (2) the ceiling grows monotonically with \(c\)
(\(9.53 < 15.01 < 20.0\) qps) so the knee shifts \emph{right}; (3) the highest
in-SLO sustained rate (\(\{2,10,20\}\) qps for \(c{=}1/2/4\)) is strictly
increasing in \(c\) and every operating point sits at \(\rho \le 1\); (4) the
stability cap holds --- throughput at the highest offered rate (40 qps) never
exceeds \(c\,\mu\); (5) the Erlang-C sojourn is a pole --- \(W\) is strictly
increasing in \(\lambda\), rises \(7.7\times\) over \(\rho{=}0.5\to0.95\), and
diverges at \(\rho{=}1\).

\paragraph{Scope (explicitly not claimed).} The \emph{absolute} knee
utilization is \emph{not} asserted. The measured per-request latency includes
the bench harness's own shell-pipeline tax (per-request \texttt{xargs}/\texttt{jq}/\texttt{awk}),
exogenous to the \(M/M/c\) service process, which depresses the single-slot
service rate in this run (\(\mu_{c=1}\approx 3.4\)/s) below its transient
ceiling (\(9.53\)/s) and shifts the apparent knee earlier for \(c{=}2,4\). Only
the \emph{scale-invariant} structure --- ceiling \(=c\,\mu\), knee shifts right,
stability cap, Erlang-C pole --- is certified; verdict
\texttt{.verdicts/sandbox/m4\_ops\_formula\_fit.txt} (5/5).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\textcolor{green!50!black}{\textbf{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL}} --- the SLO harness is
\texttt{bench/sandbox\_stage4\_slo\_full\_grid.hexa}, run cycle-16 on a
self-hosted M3 Metal mac mini (24\,GB UMA) serving
\texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-Q4\_K\_M} \citep{qwen25} through Homebrew
\texttt{llama.cpp} \citep{llamacpp} on port 8090. For each
\((\texttt{-np}, \text{rate})\) cell it boots a fresh \texttt{llama-server} at
the slot count, drives a Poisson-spaced open-loop arrival stream at the target
qps against the Stage-2 \(N{=}2000\) manifest, and records per-request wall
latency and \texttt{byte\_exact\_subset} correctness. Pre-registered protocol:
\SI{15}{\second} warmup (excluded from the percentile rollup),
\SI{60}{\second} measure window with a 2000-request budget, \(p999\) reported
only when \(n_{\text{completed}}\ge 1000\), per-request timeout \SI{30}{\second},
and a \SI{240}{\second} per-cell wall cap (cells hitting it report
\texttt{WALL\_CAPPED} percentiles over the partial drain --- an honest partial,
never a hang). Two harness hardening fixes vs the M2.OPS pilot: a wait-for-port-free
poll \(+\) 3-retry boot (\textbf{FIX-R1}, eliminating the port-teardown race ---
0/18 boot-fails vs 2/9 in the pilot) and the \SI{240}{\second} wall cap
(\textbf{FIX-R2}, replacing the pilot's \(\sim\)30-min hang). \(\texttt{-np}{=}8\)
is excluded \emph{a priori}: cycle-6 measured it 27.9\% slower than
\(\texttt{-np}{=}4\) (16\,GB-UMA memory-bandwidth saturation). The scorer is
\texttt{byte\_exact\_subset} only (no LLM self-judge, per the SANDBOX honesty
rule); transport errors (\texttt{error\_rate}, non-2xx) and content correctness
(\texttt{accuracy}) are kept strictly separate. All cells ran at \$0 incremental
on local Metal. Verdict header:
\texttt{.verdicts/sandbox/m3\_ops\_full\_slo\_grid\_summary.txt}.

\section{Benefit}
\label{sec:benefit}
\textcolor{green!50!black}{\textbf{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL}} --- the grid lands three
paper-grade, deployment-actionable deltas:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The knee shifts right with \(\texttt{-np}\) --- best-\texttt{np}
    guidance (overturns the pilot).} At \texttt{rate=5}, \(\texttt{-np}{=}2\)
    serves at p50 \SI{266}{\milli\second} where \(\texttt{-np}{=}1\) has already
    saturated (p50 \SI{4400}{\milli\second}) --- a \(16.5\times\) latency gap;
    \(\texttt{-np}{=}2\) sustains \texttt{rate=10} cleanly (VALID, full 600-req
    budget, acc 94\%) and \(\texttt{-np}{=}4\) sustains \texttt{rate=20} at p50
    \SI{1778}{\milli\second} on the full 1200-req budget. The M2.OPS pilot's
    \(\texttt{best\_np}{=}1\) was a coarse-grid artifact (its \(\{5,20,100\}\)
    grid never sampled the 5--10 qps band where extra slots win). The actionable
    rule: provision \(c\) channels to the \emph{right} of the target offered
    rate, since the sustainable rate scales roughly with \(c\).
  \item \textbf{A latency SLO violation is an \emph{accuracy} cliff --- via two
    distinct mechanisms.} (a) \emph{Client-timeout truncation}: when the long
    tail exceeds the request timeout, truncated/empty bodies score
    byte-exact-WRONG. (b) \emph{Scheduler slot-preemption} (new, \(c\ge 2\)):
    the continuous-batch scheduler preempts in-flight generations to admit new
    arrivals, returning short-but-HTTP-200 content that fails the scorer; this
    is why \(\texttt{-np}\ge 2\) accuracy collapses at \texttt{rate}\(\ge 20\)
    \emph{despite} p99 staying well under the timeout and \texttt{error\_rate}
    \(=0.00\%\). \textbf{A latency-only SLO dashboard misses both} --- the
    correctness axis is mandatory in any LLM-serving SLO.
  \item \textbf{Throughput is hard-capped at \(c\,\mu\); over-provisioning
    offered rate is pure waste.} Pushing offered rate to 40 qps never raises
    goodput above \(9.53/15.01/20.0\) qps (the measured ceilings); it buys only
    queue depth (rising p50/p99) and, past the knee, the accuracy cliff above.
    Capacity planning should target \(\rho < 1\) against \(c\,\mu\), not the
    offered-rate headline.
\end{itemize}

All three benefits are measured at \$0 incremental cost on self-hosted M3 Metal;
the harness itself --- which closes all three M2.OPS residuals (boot-race,
saturation-hang, unlocated knee) --- is part of the contribution.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The knee-shift law and why \(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\) degrades with \(c\).}
The \(c\,\mu\) ceiling proof (\S\ref{sec:formula}) predicts the knee shifts
right with the channel count; the measured ceilings \(9.53\to15.01\to20.0\) qps
confirm it. But the shift is \emph{sub-linear}: doubling \(c\) from 1 to 2 buys
\(1.57\times\) throughput, not \(2\times\), and quadrupling to 4 buys
\(2.10\times\), not \(4\times\). Read through the model, this means the
effective per-slot service rate falls,
\(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}(c)=\text{ceiling}/c = 9.53\to7.505\to5.0\) req/s/slot. The
mechanism is shared-memory contention: a 24\,GB unified-memory M3 has a single
memory-bandwidth budget, and decode is bandwidth-bound (every token streams the
model weights and the growing KV cache). Adding decode slots splits one
bandwidth budget more ways, exactly the KV-cache pressure that PagedAttention
\citep{kwon2023vllm} was designed to relieve on discrete GPUs. The practical
consequence is a diminishing-returns curve: extra slots help until the
memory-bandwidth wall, beyond which (cycle-6 measured \(\texttt{-np}{=}8\) at
\(27.9\%\) \emph{slower} than \(\texttt{-np}{=}4\)) they hurt. The \emph{shape}
--- ceiling grows, slope diminishes --- is the robust, model-certified law; the
absolute breakpoint is hardware-specific.

\paragraph{Two accuracy-cliff mechanisms, and why they are separable.} The
M2.OPS pilot saw accuracy collapse under saturation and attributed it to a
single cause --- the client timeout truncating slow completions. The full grid
shows two mechanisms that the pilot's coarse grid conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Client-timeout truncation} dominates at low \(c\) / long tail:
    when p99 \(>\) the request timeout (\SI{30}{\second}) the slowest
    completions are cut off and score byte-exact-WRONG. It is \emph{latency}
    crossing a client-side threshold. In this run it was \emph{not} triggered
    (every cell's p99 stayed \(<\SI{11.5}{\second}\)), so \(\texttt{-np}{=}1\)
    accuracy held \(86\text{--}94\%\) even when saturated.
  \item \textbf{Scheduler slot-preemption} dominates at high \(c\) / high
    concurrency: the continuous-batch scheduler admits new arrivals into a full
    batch and preempts or early-stops in-flight generations, returning
    short-but-HTTP-200 bodies. This is the OOM-avoidance / concurrency-cap
    behaviour that \citet{cheng2024scls} describe for iteration-level
    schedulers. It is \emph{content} truncation at the server, invisible to both
    latency and transport monitors.
\end{enumerate}
The separability is the operationally important part: the two mechanisms have
\emph{opposite} \(c\)-dependence (timeout-truncation eases as more slots cut the
tail; slot-preemption worsens as more slots compete for the batch), so they
cannot be collapsed into one knob. A deployment tuned to dodge (1) by adding
slots can walk straight into (2). Our grid exhibits exactly this: raising
\(\texttt{-np}\) from 1 to 4 removes the timeout tail but introduces the
preemption cliff at rate \(\ge20\) (acc \(94\to41.5\to19.3\%\)).

\paragraph{Why the correctness axis is non-optional.} In all six cliff cells
\(\texttt{error\_rate}=0.00\%\): every response was HTTP 200. A latency-and-error
dashboard --- the standard SRE pair, and the object the SLO-aware serving systems
of \S\ref{sec:related} optimise --- would show \emph{green} on both axes while
\(46\text{--}81\%\) of answers were silently wrong. The accuracy axis is the only
signal that fires. We therefore state the OPS law in correctness terms: an
offered-load SLO violation surfaces as an accuracy cliff via whichever truncation
path the deployment exposes first, and a serving SLO that omits a correctness
probe is structurally blind to the failure mode that overload actually produces.

\paragraph{Host-load invariance: reconciling the pilot.} The M2.OPS pilot
reported \(\texttt{best\_np}{=}1\) and a single-slot service rate
\(\mu_{c=1}\approx8.9\)/s; this run measures \(\mu_{c=1}\approx3.4\)/s and
\(\texttt{best\_np}\in\{2,4\}\). Both are honest. The pilot ran under light host
load so its \(\texttt{rate}{=}5\) fit under a single slot; this run's harness
drives its own per-request shell pipeline (\texttt{xargs} fan-out \(+\)
\texttt{jq} parse \(+\) \texttt{awk} keyword lookup) which competes for the same
UMA/CPU, depressing \(\mu_{c=1}\) and exposing the parallel-slot advantage. The
\emph{absolute} knee qps is thus a function of (model, box, harness overhead,
background load) and is \textbf{not} a universal constant --- which is why
\S\ref{sec:formula} certifies only the scale-invariant structure and the
recompute deliberately reads \(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\) from the data rather than
assuming it. The \emph{relative} structure --- knee shifts right with \(c\),
ceiling at \(c\,\mu\), accuracy cliff at saturation --- held in both runs and is
invariant to the absolute \(\mu\). This is the load-invariant claim, and the only
one we make.

\section{Limitations and honest caveats}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Absolute knee utilization is host-load-sensitive and not
    claimed.} The bench harness's own per-request shell pipeline competes for
    the shared UMA, depressing the single-slot service rate in this run
    (\(\mu_{c=1}\approx 3.4\)/s vs the pilot's \(\approx 8.9\)/s). Only the
    scale-invariant M/M/c structure is certified; a deployment with a different
    box, model, or arrival driver will see a different \emph{absolute} knee qps.
  \item \textbf{Single model, single box.} 0.5B Q4 on one 24\,GB-UMA M3; the
    \(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\)-degrades-with-\(c\) (memory-bandwidth) effect is
    specific to shared-memory Apple Silicon and will differ on discrete-GPU
    hosts with per-stream bandwidth.
  \item \textbf{WALL\_CAPPED cells are honest partials.} Six cells report
    percentiles over their \SI{240}{\second} partial drain; their p50/p99 are
    valid samples of the saturated regime, but \(n_{\text{completed}}\) is an
    undercount of true demand.
  \item \textbf{Open-loop, single-prompt-mix workload.} The arrival process is
    Poisson-spaced and the request distribution is the fixed Stage-2 manifest;
    we do not model bursty (closed-loop) clients, prompt-length heterogeneity,
    or the long-tail generation lengths that the SLO-aware schedulers of
    \S\ref{sec:related} target. The \(M/M/c\) exponential-service assumption is
    an idealisation of a service-time distribution that is in reality
    length-dependent; it captures the ceiling and the pole, not the exact tail
    shape.
  \item \textbf{Schematic figure is illustrative, not a data plot.}
    Figure~\ref{fig:knee} is an AI-generated schematic of the qualitative
    surface; the load-bearing numbers are the verbatim grid
    (Table~\ref{tab:grid-app}) and the recompute (Appendix~\ref{app:recompute}),
    never the artwork. Its provenance prompt is kept at
    \texttt{figures/\_prompts/knee\_shift.txt}.
\end{itemize}
