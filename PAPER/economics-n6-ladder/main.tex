\begin{abstract}
We pre-register and recompute four atlas atoms of the $n=6$ arithmetic
lattice via the \texttt{hexa verify} CLI:
$\sigma(6) = 12$, $\varphi(6) = 2$, $\tau(6) = 4$, $\sigma_2(6) = 50$.
Each recompute matches the atlas exactly and is tiered
\textsc{Supported-Formal} (closed-form / symbolic; \texttt{hexa verify}
TECS-L Tier~1). All four are persisted to
\texttt{.verdicts/economics-n6-ladder/<id>.txt} verbatim. The gate
(\texttt{cx\_paper\_gate}) opens at $4/4$ \textsc{Supported-Formal} with
zero non-blue residual, so this paper exists. Empirical contact between
these constants and any external scaling law (e.g.\ AI training compute,
inference latency) is \emph{out of scope} and lives in a separate
verification slug with T4 empirical-contact methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The $n=6$ arithmetic lattice underpins the \texttt{hexa-codex} verb
catalogue via four constants:
$\sigma(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} d$ (sum of divisors),
$\varphi(n) = \#\{k \le n : \gcd(k,n) = 1\}$ (Euler totient),
$\tau(n) = \#\{d \mid n\}$ (divisor count), and
$\sigma_2(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} d^2$ (sum of squares of divisors).
At $n = 6$ these are routinely cited as $12$, $2$, $4$, and $50$; this
artefact recomputes each from the \texttt{hexa verify --expr} calc
path and persists the verbatim stdout.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

The \texttt{hexa verify} CLI \cite{hexacodex2026} reports six tiers:
\textsc{Supported-Formal} (closed-form), \textsc{Supported-Numerical},
\textsc{Supported-By-Citation}, \textsc{Insufficient/Deferred},
\textsc{Falsified}, \textsc{Speculation-Fenced}. The gate
\texttt{cx\_paper\_gate} requires $100\%$ \textsc{Supported-Formal} ---
any other tier blocks the \texttt{/paper} scaffold.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Four claims were enumerated in \texttt{CLAIMS.tape} (one per row, each
with \texttt{method = "atom"} and a \texttt{cmd} of the form
\texttt{hexa verify --expr <fn> 6 <expected>}). Each was executed; raw
stdout was persisted verbatim to
\texttt{.verdicts/economics-n6-ladder/<id>.txt}. Re-running each
\texttt{cmd} reproduces the verdict bit-for-bit. No claim was post-hoc
downgraded or paraphrased.

\section{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0.3em
\item This artefact verifies the closed-form atlas values of the four
      $n=6$ constants. It makes no claim that these constants are
      \emph{the} exponents of any empirical AI scaling law.
\item Modeling-level claims of the form ``$\varphi(6)/\sigma(6) = 1/6$
      \emph{is} the quality\_scale loss-fit exponent'' or ``$\tau(6) =
      4$ \emph{is} the infer\_cost context exponent'' are explicitly
      out of scope here, because they cannot reach
      \textsc{Supported-Formal} from the atlas alone --- empirical AI
      curves measure different values (e.g.\ Hoffmann et al.\
      \cite{hoffmann2022chinchilla} report a loss-fit exponent
      $\alpha \approx 0.34$, not $1/6 \approx 0.167$).
\item Closing the modeling layer requires T4 empirical contact (real
      training / inference / serving benchmarks), which is a separate
      verification slug with its own claim manifest and verdict
      matrix.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion}

This paper is a verification artefact, not a scientific claim about AI
economics. Its contribution is the demonstration that the
\texttt{cx\_claim\_manifest → cx\_claim\_verify → cx\_paper\_gate}
flow produces a paper iff every claim is \textsc{Supported-Formal}.
Empirical-contact extensions (real benchmarks against transformer
training, inference, and serving) are deferred to a separate slug.
