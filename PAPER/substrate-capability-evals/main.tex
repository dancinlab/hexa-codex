\begin{abstract}
We report a capability-evaluation finding measured on a self-hosted
measurement substrate --- an \SI{0}{\$}-incremental, deterministic,
locally served stack the \texttt{hexa-codex} project uses as the shared
empirical-contact surface for all of its domains. On a stratified
\(N{=}2000\) reading-comprehension manifest evaluated across a four-rung
Qwen2.5 parameter ladder (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B), the diagnostic
\texttt{wc\_31\_60} difficulty stratum exhibits a sharp \emph{stepwise}
capability cliff: accuracy stays flat at \(\{6\%,16\%,13\%\}\) across the
three sub-7B rungs and then jumps to \(56\%\) at 7B, a single-rung lift of
\(+43\) pp. We show this curve is the wrong shape for a single-exponent
power law --- the best-per-stratum mean-accuracy log--log slope is only
\(\hat{\sigma}=0.172\), whose monotone \(3\text{B}\!\to\!7\text{B}\)
increment is just \(+2.1\) pp, one-twentieth of the measured step --- and
fit instead the simplest closed form that admits a step: a two-parameter
logistic (sigmoid) in \(\log_2(\text{params})\) with both plateaus pinned
to the data. The fit (\(k{=}8.95\), \(x_0{=}1.973\)) recomputes the
measured ladder with \(\mathrm{RMSE}=0.0356\), \emph{below} the mean
\(N{=}30\) binomial sampling-noise band of \(0.0665\); the within-plateau
scatter is therefore noise, not structural misfit. The fitted cliff
midpoint \(x_0\) lands strictly inside the 3B--7B parameter gap, matching
the empirical crossing. A hexa-native verifier recomputes all five checks
from the fitted parameters against the committed bench data
(\vfile{m4\_substrate\_formula\_fit.txt}, 5/5 PASS). Operationally, the
substrate's routing-viability flag inverts
\(\texttt{false}\!\to\!\texttt{true}\!\to\!\texttt{false}\) across the
ladder, locating cheap-persona routing arbitrage only in a narrow 1.5B--3B
mid-scale window. We close by scoping --- but explicitly not claiming ---
the multimodal capability-evaluation extension that the substrate's release
milestone names as remaining work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The shape of the capability-versus-scale curve is the central object of the
``emergent abilities'' debate in large language models. One camp reports
sharp, discontinuous jumps in downstream task accuracy as model scale
crosses some threshold \citep{wei2022emergent}; a prominent critique argues
that such ``emergence'' is largely a \emph{measurement artifact} of
discontinuous or thresholded metrics, and that under smooth, per-token
metrics the underlying capability rises continuously
\citep{schaeffer2023mirage}. Both positions are usually argued on large,
expensive, non-deterministic, API-gated model families where the
measurement surface --- the exact decoding configuration, the seed, the
scorer's tokenization --- is not under the experimenter's control.

This paper makes a narrow, fully reproducible contribution to that debate
from the opposite end of the cost spectrum. We measure a single capability
curve on a \emph{self-hosted measurement substrate}: a locally served
open-weights model ladder with seed-pinned greedy decoding, a fixed
byte-exact scorer, and a committed input manifest, all at \SI{0}{\$}
incremental cost on a consumer Apple-silicon machine. On this substrate the
curve we observe \emph{is} stepwise for one specific difficulty stratum, and
we ask the constructive question the debate often skips: \emph{if} a step is
present in the measured data, what is the simplest closed-form law that
reproduces it, and can that law be recomputed by an independent verifier
from committed numbers? Our answer is a two-parameter logistic, recomputed
to within the bench's own binomial sampling noise.

We emphasise three properties that distinguish this from a typical
scaling-law plot. First, the substrate is the contribution as much as the
finding: every number is produced by a serving process we own, so logits,
determinism, and unmetered manifest scale are all available, and re-runs are
byte-identical. Second, the paper is gated by a project rule
(\texttt{cx\_paper\_gate}) that forbids shipping any section whose claim is
not recompute-verified to a numerical (green) or formal (blue) tier; the
§Formula law here clears that gate via a hexa-native recompute, not a
narrative argument. Third, we are explicit about what the substrate does
\emph{not} yet measure --- the multimodal capability-evaluation extension
named by the release milestone is scoped in §\ref{sec:multimodal} as
remaining work, not folded into the claims.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A measured, \SI{0}{\$}-incremental, deterministic four-rung
    capability ladder on a stratified \(N{=}2000\) manifest, exhibiting a
    \(+43\) pp single-rung stepwise cliff on the \texttt{wc\_31\_60} stratum
    (§\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item A two-parameter logistic law for that cliff, with both plateaus
    pinned to the data, recomputed by an independent hexa-native verifier to
    within the bench's binomial sampling noise (§\ref{sec:formula}); and a
    structural argument, made quantitative, for why a single-exponent power
    law is the wrong functional family here.
  \item A routing-window finding: cheap-persona routing arbitrage exists
    only in a narrow mid-scale band, collapsing at both low and high
    capability (§\ref{sec:benefit}).
  \item An honest limitations section and an explicit, separately-scoped
    multimodal residual (§\ref{sec:limitations}, §\ref{sec:multimodal}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Scaling laws.} \citet{kaplan2020scaling} established smooth
power-law relationships between cross-entropy loss and model size, dataset
size, and compute; \citet{hoffmann2022chinchilla} (Chinchilla) refined the
compute-optimal allocation between parameters and tokens. These laws are
stated on \emph{loss}, a smooth per-token quantity, and are monotone and
scale-free by construction. Our §\ref{sec:formula} does not contradict them:
we fit a \emph{downstream-accuracy} curve on a thresholded task scorer,
which need not inherit the smoothness of the loss it derives from. The
contrast we draw is functional-family, not magnitude: a single power-law
exponent (\(\hat{\sigma}=0.172\) on our best-per-stratum accuracy) is
structurally monotone and cannot reproduce a step, whatever its coefficient.

\paragraph{Emergent abilities and the mirage critique.}
\citet{wei2022emergent} catalogue tasks whose accuracy is near-random until a
scale threshold and then rises sharply --- the ``emergence'' phenomenon.
\citet{schaeffer2023mirage} argue much of this is induced by discontinuous or
nonlinear metrics (e.g.\ exact-match accuracy), and that smooth surrogate
metrics dissolve the step. Our setting is squarely the kind the mirage
critique targets: our scorer is a thresholded \texttt{byte\_exact\_subset}
match, exactly the discontinuous-metric family. We do not claim the
underlying per-token capability is discontinuous; we claim only that, under
the deployed (and operationally relevant) thresholded metric, the
\emph{observed} curve on this manifest stratum is stepwise, and that a
two-parameter logistic is the minimal closed form that fits the observed
step within sampling noise. The contribution is methodological --- a
fully-recomputable, cheap, deterministic substrate on which such curves can
be fit and audited --- rather than a position in the emergence-versus-mirage
dispute. Indeed our logistic, being a smooth sigmoid, is consistent with a
continuous-underlying-capability reading: it is the smoothed step that the
thresholded metric reveals as sharp.

\paragraph{Self-hosted measurement and open weights.} The substrate serves
open-weights Qwen2.5 \citep{qwen2024} GGUF models through
\texttt{llama.cpp} \citep{llamacpp}. The methodological move --- using a
small, locally controlled, deterministic stack to measure phenomena that a
metered black-box API forecloses --- is the project's organising principle;
this paper is one instance of its capability-evaluation use.

\section{Formula}
\label{sec:formula}
\green{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL} --- the substrate's capability curve on the
\(0.5\text{B} \to 1.5\text{B} \to 3\text{B} \to 7\text{B}\) Qwen2.5
parameter ladder is \emph{stepwise}, not smooth. The diagnostic stratum
\texttt{wc\_31\_60} (the cliff feature of §\ref{sec:benchmark}) stays flat at
\(\{6\%, 16\%, 13\%\}\) across the three sub-7B rungs and then jumps to
\(56\%\) at 7B --- a \(+43\) pp single-rung lift.

\subsection{Why a single exponent is the wrong family}
A single-exponent power law \(y \propto N^{\sigma}\) is monotone and
scale-free: between any two scales \(N_1 < N_2\) its multiplicative
increment is \((N_2/N_1)^{\sigma}\), \emph{independent of where on the ladder
\(N_1\) sits}. Fitting the best-per-stratum mean accuracy across the ladder
by ordinary least squares in log--log space gives a slope
\(\hat{\sigma}=0.172\) (the same exponent recorded by the project's
empirical-landing verifier). Under that law the \(3\text{B}\to 7\text{B}\)
increment is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{y(7\text{B})}{y(3\text{B})} = \left(\frac{7}{3}\right)^{0.172}
    = 1.157,
\end{equation}
so, anchored at the measured \(y(3\text{B})=0.1333\), the predicted 7B value
is \(0.154\) --- a step of only \(+2.1\) pp. The measured step is
\(+43\) pp. No coefficient rescaling can fix this, because the shape is
wrong: a monotone power law has no place to put a cliff. This is not a fit
failure; it is a functional-family mismatch, and it is the formal reason the
emergence literature's smooth power laws on loss
\citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022chinchilla} do not transfer to a
thresholded downstream-accuracy curve with a step.

\subsection{The fitted law}
We fit the simplest closed form that admits a step --- a g0-Occam selection
over the candidate set \{single-exponent, piecewise-linear knee, Heaviside
step, logistic\}. The single-exponent is eliminated above; a Heaviside step
is a non-smooth two-parameter (location, height) law but is degenerate (it
asserts an infinitely sharp cliff and cannot express a finite transition
width); a piecewise-linear knee needs three parameters (two slopes plus a
breakpoint) and still has a derivative discontinuity. The logistic (sigmoid)
is the minimal \emph{smooth} law with a tunable transition width, and we
adopt it:
\begin{equation}
  y(x) \;=\; L_{\mathrm{lo}} \;+\;
  \frac{L_{\mathrm{hi}} - L_{\mathrm{lo}}}{1 + \exp\!\big(-k\,(x - x_0)\big)},
  \qquad x = \log_2\!\Big(\frac{N_{\mathrm{params}}}{10^{9}}\Big).
  \label{eq:logistic}
\end{equation}
The two plateaus are \emph{pinned to the data}, not free:
\(L_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0.1222\) is the mean of the three sub-7B cliff-stratum
accuracies (\(2/30,\,5/30,\,4/30\)) and \(L_{\mathrm{hi}} = 0.5667\) is the
7B value (\(17/30\)); only the cliff sharpness \(k\) and midpoint \(x_0\) are
free. Pinning the plateaus is a deliberate parsimony choice: the low and
high levels are directly observed, so letting them float would spend degrees
of freedom on quantities the data already fixes, inflating the apparent
goodness-of-fit. An RMSE grid-search over the two free parameters yields
\(k = 8.95\) and \(x_0 = 1.973\) --- a midpoint that lands strictly inside
the \(3\text{B}\,(x{=}1.585) \to 7\text{B}\,(x{=}2.807)\) gap, matching the
empirical \texttt{cliff\_crossed\_at\_7b} finding.

\subsection{Interpreting the parameters}
The sharpness \(k=8.95\) means the logistic traverses most of its rise over a
narrow \(\log_2\)-params interval: the slope at the midpoint is
\(k\,(L_{\mathrm{hi}}-L_{\mathrm{lo}})/4 \approx 0.99\) accuracy-units per
unit \(x\) (per doubling-of-doublings), i.e.\ the cliff is essentially
crossed within roughly one octave of scale around \(x_0\). The midpoint
\(x_0 = 1.973\) corresponds to \(N \approx 2^{1.973}\,\text{B} \approx
3.9\,\text{B}\) parameters --- a prediction that the true crossover SKU lies
near 4B, between the measured 3B and 7B rungs. This is a falsifiable,
substrate-checkable prediction: landing a 4B Qwen2.5 rung (an existing SKU,
not yet on disk) would test whether the cliff midpoint is where the logistic
places it (§\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Residual}
The logistic recompute (\texttt{verify/numerics\_substrate\_cliff\_\allowbreak
logistic.hexa}) reproduces the measured ladder with RMSE \(0.0356\), which is
\emph{below} the mean \(N{=}30\) binomial sampling-noise band of \(0.0665\)
--- i.e.\ the within-plateau scatter (\(-5.6\) pp at 0.5B, \(+4.4\) pp at
1.5B) is smaller than its own per-point sampling standard error and is
therefore noise, not structural misfit. The recomputed
\(3\text{B}\to 7\text{B}\) step is \(+0.4308\) vs the observed \(+0.4333\)
(drift \(0.0025\)). All five checks pass; verdict
\vfile{m4\_substrate\_formula\_fit.txt}. The full per-point residual table and
the verbatim recompute transcript are reproduced in Appendix
\ref{app:recompute}; Figure \ref{fig:cliff-data} overlays the fitted law on
the measured points with binomial error bars, and Figure
\ref{fig:cliff-schematic} gives a schematic of the same contrast.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\green{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL} --- the measurement substrate is a self-hosted M3
Metal mac mini running \texttt{llama-server} (Homebrew \texttt{llama.cpp}
HEAD) dispatching open-weights Qwen2.5 GGUF base models through
\texttt{lm\_foundry/tool/route\_dispatch.hexa} (commit \texttt{b376590}). The
scale ladder is fully on disk: 0.5B (\texttt{cycle-8 bfd2885}), 1.5B
(\texttt{cycle-8 008482e}), 3B (\texttt{cycle-10 56aae56}), 7B
(\texttt{cycle-12 dc9f4fa}); all Q4\_K\_M quantised, each with a committed
SHA256 (Appendix \ref{app:rungs}).

\paragraph{Determinism.} Cycle-4 \texttt{d\_kv\_prefix\_share} established
bit-identical cache replay at \texttt{top-k=1 / temp=0 / seed-pinned}, so
every rung's bench is reproducible byte-for-byte; this is the property the
emergence-versus-mirage debate \citep{schaeffer2023mirage} typically cannot
assume on a metered API. Cycle-5 \texttt{395936e} fixed the
\texttt{byte\_exact\_subset} scorer's stderr-trailer-strip bug, eliminating a
\(\sim\)3 pp accuracy inflation observed on Stage 0; the SCORER-FIXED scorer
is used uniformly across all four rungs here. No self-judging occurs: the
substrate LLM never grades substrate-LLM output, per the project's honesty
rule.

\paragraph{Persona grid and sampling.} Three personas span a token-budget
grid: \texttt{nano} (system prompt ``Answer in \(\le\)15 tokens'', 32-token
cap), \texttt{mid} (``Answer concisely'', 256-token cap), and \texttt{max}
(``Answer carefully and thoroughly'', 1024-token cap), all at temperature
\(0\). The personas mock a 1:5:25 vendor price ratio; only the \emph{ratio}
of decode budgets matters for the routing analysis, not absolute price. Each
rung draws 30 tasks per stratum at stride 13 from the committed \(N{=}2000\)
manifest, across 5 word-count strata (\texttt{wc\_5\_15}, \texttt{wc\_16\_30},
\texttt{wc\_31\_60}, \texttt{wc\_61\_100}, \texttt{wc\_101\_200}), for
\(150\) tasks \(\times\,3\) personas \(=450\) bench rows per rung and
\(1800\) rows total. The cliff analysis of §\ref{sec:formula} uses the
\texttt{nano} persona on \texttt{wc\_31\_60}.

\paragraph{Cost.} All four rungs ran at \SI{0}{\$} incremental on M3 Metal
(\(\sim\)66--80 min total wall-clock across cycles). The eval input set is
registered as the public HF dataset
\texttt{dancinlab/hexa-codex-sandbox-evals-v1} (persona-ladder cases, 450
rows), and every per-rung verdict cross-tab is committed under
\vfile{}. Cross-link: \texttt{SUBSTRATE.log.md} 2026-05-24 entry (commit
\texttt{37f745c}).

\section{Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
\green{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL} --- the Stage 2 \(N{=}2000\) stratified manifest
(cycle-5 \texttt{d\_stage2\_scale\_manifest}, commit \texttt{91ac831}) has 5
word-count strata of 400 tasks each, generated deterministically with
SHA256-identical re-runs. All 4 rungs ran FULL (commit \texttt{71b4288},
verdict \texttt{m3\_substrate\_saturation\_summary.txt}). The headline result
is the 4-rung mean-accuracy ladder \(\{0.34, 0.42, 0.42, 0.59\}\) plus the
per-stratum cliff matrix (nano persona):

\begin{center}

\toprule
stratum         & 0.5B  & 1.5B  & 3B    & 7B \\
\midrule
\texttt{wc\_5\_15}     & 73\% & 96\% & 96\% & 96\% \\
\texttt{wc\_16\_30}    & 86\% & 96\% & 96\% & 100\% \\
\texttt{wc\_31\_60}    & 6\%  & 16\% & 13\% & \textbf{56\%} \\
\texttt{wc\_61\_100}   & 0\%  & 16\% & 3\%  & 30\% \\
\texttt{wc\_101\_200}  & 0\%  & 3\%  & 6\%  & 10\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \texttt{wc\_31\_60} ``cliff'' is crossed (50\% threshold) only between 3B
and 7B (\(+43\) pp single-rung lift). The two easiest strata
(\texttt{wc\_5\_15}, \texttt{wc\_16\_30}) are already near-saturated at 0.5B

an upper ceiling on substrate capability under the manifest's
reading-comprehension difficulty distribution. The \texttt{wc\_31\_60}
stratum is thus the unique \emph{diagnostic} stratum: low enough that small
models fail it, high enough that 7B clears it, so it carries the entire scale
signal and is the feature the §\ref{sec:formula} law is fit to. Full
per-rung, per-persona cross-tabs (correct/\(n\), accuracy, wall-time) are in
Appendix \ref{app:perrung}.

\section{Benefit}
\label{sec:benefit}
\green{SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL} --- the 4-rung ladder lands two paper-grade
benefit deltas.

\paragraph{(1) Stepwise cliff is paper-grade and decision-useful.} The
\texttt{wc\_31\_60} cliff jumps \(+43\) pp between 3B and 7B but stays flat
(\(\pm 3\) pp) between 0.5B and 3B. For a practitioner choosing a model size
for a deployment whose task distribution resembles \texttt{wc\_31\_60}, the
substrate delivers a clean, \SI{0}{\$}-cost result: \emph{every sub-7B rung
uniformly fails the middle stratum on this manifest}, so there is no
``buy a slightly bigger small model'' path --- the capability is gated behind
the 3B--7B step, and a logistic with \(x_0\approx 3.9\text{B}\) predicts
where (§\ref{sec:formula}). The single-exponent baseline would have wrongly
told the practitioner that a 3B-to-7B upgrade buys only \(+2\) pp.

\paragraph{(2) Routing-window is narrow.} The substrate's
\texttt{routing\_simulation\_viable} flag inverts
\(\texttt{false} \to \texttt{true} \to \texttt{false}\) across
\(\{0.5\text{B}, 1.5\text{B/3B}, 7\text{B}\}\):
\begin{itemize}
  \item At \textbf{0.5B}, all three personas fail uniformly (persona spread
    \(=4\) tasks, ranking driven by noise) --- the model is below the
    capability floor, so there is nothing to route.
  \item At \textbf{1.5B--3B}, the cheap-persona-on-easy-task arbitrage opens
    (spread \(=8\) tasks; the cheap \texttt{nano} persona ranks first on
    overall accuracy, \(46\%\) vs \(40\%\) for \texttt{mid}/\texttt{max}) ---
    the routing sweet spot.
  \item At \textbf{7B}, the cheapest persona already saturates the easy
    strata and converges with the expensive ones (spread collapses to 2;
    nano/mid/max overall \(58/60/58\%\)) --- routing differentiation
    vanishes.
\end{itemize}
This means routing-economics claims (cf.\
\texttt{PAPER/economics-routing-savings/}) must \emph{condition} their
benefit on this mid-scale window: the same router that saves money at
1.5B--3B saves nothing at 7B (the cheap persona is already good enough) and
nothing at 0.5B (no persona is good enough). The substrate makes this
condition measurable rather than assumed.

Both findings are measured at \SI{0}{\$} incremental cost per rung
(self-hosted M3 Metal); the substrate itself --- a deterministic,
owned-process, unmetered surface --- is the contribution as much as the
findings.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
We are explicit about scope.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Four rungs only.} The ladder is
    \(\{0.5,1.5,3,7\}\text{B}\); the cliff is bracketed to the 3B--7B
    interval but the exact crossover is not directly observed. The logistic
    places \(x_0\approx 3.9\text{B}\); a 4B Qwen2.5 SKU exists and is not yet
    on disk. Landing it would tighten the cliff bracket and directly test the
    \(x_0\) prediction --- the single most informative next measurement.
  \item \textbf{Single eval family.} The manifest is one
    reading-comprehension difficulty distribution stratified by word count.
    The cliff is a statement about \emph{this} family; we do not claim it
    generalises to reasoning, code, or math benchmarks. The substrate makes
    adding eval families cheap, but each is a separate measurement.
  \item \textbf{Thresholded metric.} Per the mirage critique
    \citep{schaeffer2023mirage}, the \texttt{byte\_exact\_subset} scorer is
    discontinuous; the observed step is a property of the deployed metric, not
    necessarily of per-token capability. We fit the observed curve and say so
    (§\ref{sec:related}); a smooth-surrogate re-measurement is future work.
  \item \textbf{\(N{=}30\) per stratum.} The per-point binomial SE is
    4.3--9.1 pp; the fit is within that band, but tightening it (larger
    per-stratum \(n\)) would sharpen \(k\) and \(x_0\). The cliff
    magnitude (\(+43\) pp) is many SEs and is robust; the precise sharpness
    is the noisier parameter.
  \item \textbf{Quantisation.} All rungs are Q4\_K\_M; the curve is for the
    quantised models actually served, not full-precision weights.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Multimodal residual (release milestone, not claimed)}
\label{sec:multimodal}
The substrate's release milestone (M5.SUBSTRATE) names ``multimodal
\(+\) capability-eval landing''. This paper lands the \emph{text}
capability-eval half (§\ref{sec:benchmark}, §\ref{sec:formula}) but
explicitly does \textbf{not} land the multimodal half, which is heavy new
scope. We scope it here as remaining work rather than claim it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A multimodal model on the substrate.} The current ladder is
    text-only Qwen2.5 GGUF served by \texttt{llama.cpp}. A multimodal rung
    (e.g.\ a vision-language Qwen2.5-VL SKU) requires a serving path that
    accepts image inputs with the same determinism guarantees (seed-pinned,
    byte-exact replay) the text path has --- a non-trivial serving-stack
    extension, not a config change.
  \item \textbf{A new eval family with a deterministic scorer.} A multimodal
    capability eval needs an image-grounded task manifest and a scorer that
    is byte-exact (no self-judging), stratified by a difficulty axis
    analogous to \texttt{wc} so a cliff (if any) can be located. Building and
    committing that manifest is comparable in effort to the text Stage-2
    manifest.
  \item \textbf{What ``done'' would mean.} The multimodal half lands only
    when a multimodal rung produces a recompute-verified (green/blue)
    capability curve under the same \texttt{cx\_paper\_gate} bar this paper
    meets --- i.e.\ a closed-form fit recomputed by a hexa-native verifier
    against committed multimodal-bench data.
\end{itemize}
Because this is genuine new scope, the M5.SUBSTRATE milestone remains open
pending the multimodal landing even with the text half published; this paper
is the text-capability-eval deliverable, not the full release.
